## The "Before" Era (Slide 3-4)
Before AI, development was hard. Every single line was manual. You spent more time on Stack Overflow than in your actual editor. We had specialized teams for everything, which created knowledge silos and long release cycles.

The problems were persistent:
- High entry barriers (3-5 years to be productive).
- 70% of time wasted on boilerplate.
- Documentation that was obsolete the moment it was written.
- A "Bus Factor" of 1, where if one person left, the whole system collapsed.

Status: **Frustrating.**

## The Trap: Vibe Coding (Slide 8-10)
Many developers fall into the trap of "Vibe Coding." You write a vague spec, AI generates the code, you don't check it, and it seems to work. 

But soon, **Software Entropy** sets in. The code starts to rot. Without intentional design, AI will just amplify the chaos.

Matt Pocock’s central thesis is this: **The developers who succeed with AI aren't those who delegate everything. They are those who apply software engineering fundamentals TO the AI.**

## The 5 Fundamental Principles (Slide 11-16)

### 01. "Grill Me"
Don't just give a prompt. Ask Claude to interrogate your specs. Reach a shared conceptual design before a single line of code is written.

### 02. Ubiquitous Language
Define your domain. Create a `vocabulary.md` file. Ensure you and the AI speak the same language to avoid inconsistent naming.

### 03. TDD (Test-Driven Development)
The test IS the spec. Write the test first, then let AI make it pass. This creates a perfect, measurable feedback loop.

### 04. Deep Modules
Keep interfaces simple and implementations complex (hidden). AI understands deep, well-defined modules much better than shallow ones with 20 parameters.

### 05. Design vs Implementation
You are the **Strategist**. AI is the **Tactician**. You decide the *What* and the *Why*; let the AI figure out the *How*.
